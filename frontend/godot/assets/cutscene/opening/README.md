# Opening assets

Original project art and synthesized audio, generated by
`python3 frontend/godot/tools/build_opening_assets.py` from the repository root.
No third-party images, sound recordings or generated-image services were used.
No external asset license is required. The existing fonts retain their OFL license.

The generator reuses only the original pixel-kit drawing definitions and player
palette; it does not regenerate unrelated assets. Ship sources are 160×192,
recovery poses are 24×32 (feet 12,30), and coast textures are 16×16. Source pixels
occupy two world units, with nearest filtering. Two authored ship headings and
five discrete falling-mast poses avoid interpolated sprite rotation. Recovery,
brace, stand, climb, fall and looking have dedicated poses; final walking uses the
existing walk frames, advanced by cinematic time.

WAVs are deterministic, original 22,050 Hz mono 16-bit synthesis: separate wind,
surf, creak, low grounding impact, hull crack, mast, splash, cloth, step and bird.
They remain at their authored pitch at 2×. The director schedules starts and the
blackout mix boundary stops voices; no dialogue or melodic score is included.
These are stylized synthetic effects, not recordings of a real wooden ship.

Second pass (2026-10-13): `tools/build_south_assets.py`, also invoked by the
opening generator, owns the final warm sand / wet sand / turquoise shallows,
seven irregular fog silhouettes (160–240 × 96–128 source pixels), four palms
and four broadleaf trees (96×112, foot pivot 48,106), three variants each of
shrubs, flowers, shells, stones, driftwood, outcrops, fallen trees and trail
markers. Small stones/shells are 32×32; other details are 64×64. Their ground
pivots are `(width/2,height-6)`; shadows are inside the same uncut sprite.
Trunk/landmark footprints are data in `scripts/south_scenery.gd`; canopies and
small ground details remain non-solid. Source pixels use two world units and
nearest sampling throughout. SVGs contain only integer pixel-color runs.

The user-supplied beach image informs density, palette and layered composition.
No pixels, watermark or decorative pencil were copied. All new art is original
code-authored pixel art, with no third-party asset dependencies or licensing
restrictions. Reviewed in native Godot Metal captures at the actual pixel scale;
see `docs/evidence/starting-cutscene/second-pass/` for current evidence.
